## The problem

Before AI:
- Remote meetings ran over time, lost focus, and people left with different understandings because of second-language English.
- Sprint planning was slow: [[o que acontecia? ex. horas a escrever tarefas no Jira]]
- Existing codebases had little or no documentation: [[impacto concreto: ex. onboarding lento, dependência de 1–2 pessoas]]
- The company had AI tools available, but [[quantas pessoas usavam? quase ninguém?]]

## What I did

1. **Started from the pain, not the tool.** Asked each person what wasted their time every week.
2. **First-start sessions, one person at a time** (30–45 min on their real work), then a 10-minute follow-up a few days later.
3. **Meetings first:** AI-drafted agenda before, transcript during, a clear, plain-English summary after, reviewed by a human.
4. **Then planning and Jira:** draft tasks and acceptance criteria, Jira automation, preparation of sprint planning.
5. **Then the development cycle:** prototypes, documentation of 5+ existing codebases, AI-assisted tests and first-pass code review.
6. **Guardrails before scaling:** [[que regras havia? ferramentas aprovadas, dados que não podiam entrar]]

## Lessons for other project managers

1. The first real task, done together, matters more than any training.
2. AI drafts, humans decide: every output has a named owner.
3. In multilingual remote teams, the written summary is the product of the meeting.
