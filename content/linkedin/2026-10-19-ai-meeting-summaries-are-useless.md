Most AI meeting summaries are useless. Here is why.

Nobody owns them.

The tool records the call, writes two pages, and sends them to everyone. Nobody reads them. Nobody checks them. A week later, the team argues about what was decided.

The mistake is treating the summary as the product of the tool. It is the product of the meeting, and the meeting needs a person in charge of it.

With remote teams across time zones, most working in English as a second language, this is what worked for me:

→ Before: AI drafts the agenda from the open topics
→ During: AI transcribes, so people can focus on the conversation
→ After: one named person turns it into a short, plain-English summary: decisions, owners, dates

That human step is short. It is also the part people actually read.

The result: meetings that stay on time, better focus, and fewer language misunderstandings.

Who owns the summary in your team's meetings today?
