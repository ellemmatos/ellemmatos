Being the only woman in the room is common. Being unheard should not be.

In technical meetings, the problem is rarely a lack of ideas. It is that some voices get interrupted, skipped, or repeated later by someone else who gets the credit.

Fixing that is not only the job of the person who is unheard. It is also the job of whoever runs the meeting.

If you are the one speaking:

→ Prepare one clear point before the meeting
→ Say it in the first ten minutes
→ If someone repeats your idea, connect it back: "Yes, that builds on what I proposed earlier"

If you are leading the meeting:

→ Ask each person for input, in turn, before the open discussion
→ Credit ideas by name in the summary
→ Rotate who facilitates

Small habits like these change who is heard, without anyone having to raise their voice.

I lead remote technical teams and speak about diversity in the workplace. These are the habits I recommend most.

Which habit has made the biggest difference in your meetings?
