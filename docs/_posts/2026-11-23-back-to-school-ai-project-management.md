I use AI in my projects every week. It helps prepare meetings, plan work in Jira, write tests, review code and document existing codebases.

But using AI to deliver a project is not the same as delivering an AI project. I have not yet led the delivery of an AI product. That is the next step I want to take: becoming a project manager for AI initiatives.

So I am going back to school. And I am doing it in public, so you can follow along and hold me accountable.

## Using AI in a project is not managing an AI project

When a team uses AI to work faster, the project itself does not change much. The scope is still a website, an integration or a migration. AI is a tool in the process.

When the product is AI, the project changes. These are my working assumptions, and the course will test them:

- **The output is not fixed.** The same input can give a different answer. "Done" is harder to define.
- **Data comes first.** Without the right data, there is no project, however good the plan looks.
- **Some work is an experiment.** You may not know if an approach works until you try it.

As a project manager, I know how to plan scope, timelines and client expectations. What I want to learn is how those tools change when the outcome is uncertain.

## Where I start

I am not starting from zero, and I am not pretending to be further than I am.

- I lead remote engineering teams that work AI-first, from planning to code.
- Before project management, I was a senior .NET and SharePoint developer. That background lets me discuss architecture with tech leads as a peer.
- I have coached people on my teams from their first AI use to working with it on their own.

What I do not have yet is experience leading an AI product from idea to production. That is the gap this series is about.

## The plan

I am starting with a free course from PMI: **"PMI Essentials: Seven AI Project Patterns"**. It looks at AI work through a small set of recurring project patterns. The idea is to recognise what kind of AI project you have before you plan it.

My plan is simple:

1. **Two weeks, six short sessions.** Small enough to fit around a full-time job.
2. **One post per pattern, once a week.** What I learned, in plain words, and where I see that pattern in my own work, or where I would use it.
3. **Then the next step:** the free introduction to PMI's CPMAI certification.

I will only write about what I have actually studied. If I do not know something yet, I will say so.

## What each post in the series will cover

To keep the series useful, every pattern post will answer the same questions:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What is this pattern, in one sentence? | You should be able to explain it to a client |
| Where does it show up in real work? | A pattern you cannot recognise is useless |
| What should a project manager ask before starting? | This is where projects are won or lost |
| What surprised me? | The honest part of learning |

You can use the same table for your own study, whatever course you take.

## Why learn in public

Three reasons.

**It keeps me consistent.** A weekly post is a deadline. Deadlines work.

**It makes the learning better.** Explaining something simply is the best test of whether you understood it.

**It helps others on the same path.** Many people in delivery roles use AI tools every day and are wondering what it takes to lead AI work. If my notes save someone a few hours, that is worth it.

## How you can help

Two ways.

First, tell me what kind of AI project your company is running. A chatbot? Document processing? Predictions from data? Real examples will make this series better than any course alone.

Second, hold me accountable. If a week goes by without a pattern post, ask me where it is.

## The takeaway

Using AI well in your projects is a good start, but it is not the same as managing AI projects. I am closing that gap one pattern at a time, in public, starting this week.

If you lead teams that are starting with AI, my free [AI Adoption Playbook for Engineering Teams](https://github.com/ellemmatos/ellemmatos/blob/main/playbook/ai-adoption-playbook.md) covers the part I already know: bringing AI into how a team works.
